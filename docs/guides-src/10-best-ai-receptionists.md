A competitor wins this one outright. Smith.ai offers an AI receptionist plan at $0 that covers 25 calls a month, as of August 2026, per their pricing page. ### Best rock-bottom budget voice: My AI Front Desk or Dialzara

If your budget is measured in tens of dollars, My AI Front Desk starts at $20 a month and Dialzara starts at $29, as of August 2026, per vendor pricing pages. Both are legitimate ways to get an AI answering your phone for less than most people spend on coffee. The caveat is metered billing: My AI Front Desk's fuller bundle is $99 a month with 200 voice minutes and $0.25 per minute after that, and Dialzara's tiers run up to $349 depending on minutes, with SMS and chat as paid add-ons. A quiet month is cheap. A busy month is not, so estimate your minutes honestly before you assume the sticker price is your price.

### Best simple flat-fee phone answering: Goodcall

If the phone is your only channel that matters, Goodcall is a strong pick: $79 to $249 per agent with unlimited minutes, as of August 2026, per their pricing page. Unlimited minutes means your bill does not jump when your phone gets busy, which is worth a lot for budgeting peace of mind. The tiers are priced by unique callers, and it is phone-focused, so if your customers also text or message you on social channels, you will be stitching those together separately.

### Best if you want a human voice: Smith.ai's human receptionists or Ruby

AI is not the answer to every phone, and anyone selling one should say so. If your callers are often upset, anxious, or dealing with sensitive matters, say a law office handling difficult intakes, a human's judgment and warmth still beat any script an AI follows. Smith.ai's human virtual receptionists run $300 to $2,100 a month, and Ruby runs from $250 for 50 minutes up to $1,725 for 500 minutes, which works out to roughly $3.45 to $5 per minute, as of August 2026, per vendor pricing pages. ## How to actually choose

Five questions will get you further than any ranking, ours included.

**1. Which channels do your customers actually use?** Check your last month of inquiries. If everything comes by phone, a phone-only tool like Goodcall is plenty. If a salon's bookings arrive by Instagram DM and a real estate lead texts at 9 pm, a phone-only receptionist answers the wrong door.

**2. What's your real monthly volume?** Count calls and messages for two weeks, then double it. Metered plans look cheap until you run the numbers at your actual volume, and flat plans look expensive until you do the same.

**3. Who owns the number and the data if you leave?** Ask every vendor what happens to your phone number, your conversation history, and your customer list on the day you cancel. With most services the answer is that some or all of it stays behind. This is the question we built our whole model around, but it is worth asking no matter who you buy from.

**4. What's the year-two price?** Some prices stay flat forever. Some, like ours, drop after 12 payments. Some creep upward with usage, add-ons, and per-minute overages. Compare 24-month totals, not first-month stickers.

**5. Can you test it before buying?** Never buy an AI receptionist you haven't talked to. Ours is open to anyone with no signup at the [Velonyx Playground](https://velonyx-playground.vercel.app), where you can call, text, or chat with a real AI receptionist right now. And Smith.ai's free plan is a fair way to test theirs. Any vendor who won't let you try the product is answering question five for you.

## FAQ

### What's the single best AI receptionist overall?

Honestly, that is the wrong question, and any article that answers it with one product name is selling you something. The best pick for a fitness studio getting 40 DMs a day is different from the best pick for an accounting firm getting six calls a week. Start with your channels, your volume, and whether ownership matters to you, then use the table above. The "overall winner" framing exists because it is easy to write, not because it helps you buy.

### What's the cheapest AI receptionist that's actually good?

For voice on a tight budget, My AI Front Desk at $20 a month and Dialzara from $29 are the real answers, as of August 2026, per vendor pricing pages, as long as you keep an eye on metered minutes. Smith.ai's free plan is the cheapest possible start at $0 for 25 calls. If you want cheap and predictable rather than cheap and metered, Goodcall's $79 tier with unlimited minutes is the safer bet.

### Are the AI voices actually believable in 2026?

They have gotten good enough that many callers get through a whole booking without friction, but "believable" is not really the goal, and attentive callers can still tell. Our AI identifies itself as an AI on every call, on purpose, because pretending otherwise is a bad way to open a relationship with a customer. The only honest way to judge voice quality is to hear it yourself: call one in the [playground](https://velonyx-playground.vercel.app) and decide with your own ears.

### Should a very small business just use voicemail?

Sometimes, yes. If you get a few calls a week, return them the same day, and your customers are patient, a clear voicemail greeting costs nothing and works fine. An AI receptionist starts earning its keep when calls come in while you are with customers, when people want to book outside business hours, or when inquiries arrive on channels you cannot watch all day. If none of that describes you yet, keep your money.
