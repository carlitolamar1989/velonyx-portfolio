Restaurant phones have a scheduling problem no other business quite shares: they ring hardest at the exact moments nobody can answer them. Friday at 7pm the dining room is full, every server is mid-table, and the person closest to the host stand is carrying three plates. The phone rings anyway.

An AI receptionist for restaurants answers that phone every time it rings, including the four calls that land at once during the rush. This guide walks through what restaurant calls actually are, which ones deserve the most care, how an AI answering service fits (and doesn't fit) alongside OpenTable or Resy, and what it honestly costs.

## What restaurant calls actually are

Most restaurant calls are short questions with fast answers. That's exactly why they're frustrating to staff mid-service: each one is easy, but each one pulls a person away from a table.

| The call | How an AI receptionist handles it |
| --- | --- |
| "Are you open Monday?" "Where do we park?" | Answers instantly from the hours, location, and parking info you configure |
| "Do you have gluten-free options?" | Answers from the menu information you give it, including allergens and substitutions you've spelled out |
| "How long's the wait right now?" | Reports whatever you've told it to say, and only that (more on this below) |
| "Can you fit a party of 12 Saturday?" | Routes it as a booking or takes a detailed message, depending on how you set it up |
| "Do you do private events? Catering?" | Runs intake questions and books a call-back on your calendar |

One honest note on wait times, because this is where a restaurant AI can quietly overpromise. The AI cannot see your floor. It knows what you've told it, so the right setup is an honest standing answer, something like "weekend waits typically run 30 to 45 minutes, and we'll text you when your table is close." It should set expectations, not invent a number, and a good vendor will say so up front.

Large-party requests deserve their own rule. A 12-top on a Saturday isn't a normal booking, so you decide the routing: the AI can book it into a slot you've defined, or capture the details and flag it for a human to confirm. Either way the caller gets a real answer instead of a ring-out.

## The calls worth the most: private events and catering

A rehearsal dinner for 40. An office holiday party. Weekly catering for a job site. These calls come in on the same line as "are you open Monday," and they're worth many times more, which makes them the strongest single argument for answering every call well.

An AI receptionist can run a proper intake on these: date, headcount, budget range, occasion, and contact details. Then, instead of promising that someone will call back eventually, it books a call-back or a site-visit slot directly on the owner's Google Calendar during the conversation and texts a confirmation. The lead arrives on your phone as a scheduled appointment with notes, not a voicemail.

That workflow is worth testing before you buy anything. Call a vendor's demo line, pretend to be planning a party for 35, and see whether you end the call with an actual time on a calendar.

## The honest fit: if your reservations live on OpenTable, Resy, or Toast

Here's the part most vendor pages skip. If you run reservations through OpenTable, Resy, Toast, or a similar system, an AI receptionist does not replace that book, and you shouldn't want it to. Velonyx books appointments onto your Google Calendar, which is a different tool than a per-table reservation system that manages turn times, table assignments, and cover counts. That management stays where it is.

So what's left for the AI to do in a reservation-system restaurant? Everything around the book, which is most of what your phone actually carries:

| Your setup | Where an AI receptionist fits |
| --- | --- |
| Reservations on OpenTable, Resy, or Toast | Answers the questions, texts callers your reservation link so they book themselves, captures event and catering leads, covers every hour you're closed |
| Counter service, food trucks, small BYOB spots | The calendar booking is the whole fit: the AI books tastings, pickups, and reservations directly |
| Event-driven rooms (private dining, catering-first) | Intake plus a call-back or site visit booked straight onto your calendar |

For reservation-light spots, the math is simpler. A food truck taking catering inquiries, a counter-service spot booking large-group pickups, a 30-seat BYOB that just needs a name and a time: for these, booking onto a calendar mid-call is exactly the job.

And to say it plainly: if all you want is after-hours question answering and a texted reservation link, you may only need part of what a full AI front desk does. A cheaper tier, or a leaner product, might serve you fine. An honest vendor should tell you that on the first call.

## Phone first, then the DMs

Restaurants are a phone-first industry, and that shapes which product tier makes sense. Voice is the expensive, hard-to-do-well channel, and it's the one that rings during service. If the phone is your problem, buy the phone solution.

Instagram is the quiet second channel. Diners DM restaurants the same questions they call about, usually at 10pm while looking at your food photos, and those messages sit unanswered for the same staffing reasons the phone does. An AI that answers DMs with the same configured information closes that gap without anyone standing at the pass checking a phone.

Texting rounds it out: confirmations for booked call-backs, your reservation link sent mid-call, and a follow-up if an event lead goes quiet. One more thing worth a sentence: the AI can hold these conversations in multiple languages, which matters when a real share of your callers are more comfortable in Spanish or another language. And by design it always identifies itself as an AI, so nobody feels tricked into thinking they reached a person.

## FAQ

### Does it integrate with OpenTable or Resy?

Not in the sense of writing reservations into your OpenTable or Resy book, and you should be skeptical of any vendor who's vague about this. Velonyx books onto your Google Calendar, so per-table reservation management stays in your reservation system where it belongs.

What it does instead: answers the phone and DM questions around the book, texts callers your reservation link so they complete the booking themselves, captures event and catering leads with real intake, and covers every hour you're closed.

### Can it take takeout orders over the phone?

Taking orders and pushing them into a POS is a different integration than answering the phone, and it's worth being direct about that. Out of the box, the AI can answer menu questions, text the caller your online ordering link, or take a message for the kitchen, depending on how you configure it.

If full phone ordering into your POS is the main thing you need, raise it on the first call so it gets scoped honestly rather than promised loosely.

### What happens when five calls come in at once during the dinner rush?

They all get answered. The AI isn't one person at a host stand; it's software that holds each conversation separately, so three calls at 7:04 become three simultaneous conversations instead of a hold queue. This is the one thing no amount of front-of-house staffing can replicate, because you'd never hire five people to sit by the phone for a 20-minute spike.

### Can it speak Spanish?

Yes. The AI can hold conversations in multiple languages, including Spanish, and you configure which languages it offers. For the calls restaurants actually get, hours, menu questions, and bookings, it converses naturally, though you should test it in your language of choice on the playground before relying on it.
